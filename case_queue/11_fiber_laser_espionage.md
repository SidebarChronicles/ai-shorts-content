# 11 — The Fiber-Laser Engineer Who Stole DARPA Weapons Research

**Status:** QUEUED
**Narrative score:** 9/10 — military tech, $11.4M DARPA project, Thousand Talents
**Source:** [DOJ press release, November 5, 2025](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/fiber-laser-expert-convicted-federal-jury-economic-espionage-and-theft-trade-secrets)
**Defendant:** Ji Wang, 63, Painted Post NY (Corning Inc. engineer)
**Court:** U.S. District Court, Western District of New York (Judge Frank P. Geraci, Jr.)
**Sentence:** Convicted Nov 4, 2025 — sentencing April 15, 2026 (faces 15 yrs per economic-espionage count + 10 yrs per trade-secret count)
**Conviction basis:** Federal jury — 2 counts economic espionage + 1 theft of trade secrets + 1 attempted economic espionage + 1 attempted theft of trade secrets

## Hook

DARPA spent eleven million dollars to build laser weapons that could shoot down drones. He stole the manufacturing files. He planned to sell the tech to China — for use on tanks.

## Arc

Ji Wang, 63, immigrated to the U.S. in 1998 to work for Corning Inc. From 2002 to 2007, he worked on a 5-year, $11.4 million DARPA project to develop high-powered fiber laser optical fibers — the goal was lasers that could shoot down drones and missiles. On July 1, 2016, Wang stole hundreds of files containing the trade-secret manufacturing technology. Ten days earlier, he had applied for China's Thousand Talents Plan Award (a PRC initiative paying millions to overseas Chinese scientists to return). Two months after the theft, he was selected. From 2014-2017, Wang negotiated with Chinese government entities to start a specialty-fiber business in China funded by tens of millions of dollars. His business plans pitched military applications — he wrote that specialty fibers "can also be installed on military vehicles," including tanks, and could "be key to deciding victory or defeat." Law enforcement disrupted the plan before he launched. Federal jury convicted November 2025.

## Key quotes

> "A federal jury has convicted Ji Wang, 63, of Painted Post, N.Y., of two counts of economic espionage, one count of theft of trade secrets, one count of attempted economic espionage, and one count of attempted theft of trade secrets."

> "Between 2002 and 2007, Wang was assigned to work on a joint research and development project funded by the Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency ('DARPA') and Corning. The goal of the 5-year, $11.4 million project was to develop optical fibers for high-powered lasers with military and commercial applications. DARPA and Corning aimed to increase the power of fiber lasers by more than a factor of 1000. DARPA sought to develop this technology to create laser weapons capable of shooting down drones and missiles."

> "On or about July 1, 2016, Wang stole hundreds of files that contained non-public data generated during the DARPA project, including trade-secret manufacturing technology that would have enabled him to fabricate all manner of specialty optical fibers, including for fiber lasers."

> "Ten days before Wang stole the trade secret files, he had applied for China's Thousand Talents Plan Award."

> "In one such business plan, which Wang submitted to a Chinese government entity, he advertised that specialty fibers 'can also be installed on military vehicles,' including 'tanks.' Wang claimed that such use of the technology on military vehicles could 'be key to deciding victory or defeat.'"
