# 09 — The Google Engineer Who Stole AI Secrets For China

**Status:** QUEUED
**Narrative score:** 10/10 — first-ever AI economic espionage conviction
**Source:** [DOJ press release, January 30, 2026](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/former-google-engineer-found-guilty-economic-espionage-and-theft-confidential-ai-technology)
**Defendant:** Linwei "Leon" Ding, 38
**Court:** U.S. District Court, Northern District of California (Judge Vince Chhabria)
**Sentence:** Convicted January 29, 2026 — sentencing pending (faces 10 yrs per trade-secret count + 15 yrs per economic-espionage count = potentially decades)
**Conviction basis:** Federal jury after 11-day trial — 7 counts economic espionage + 7 counts theft of trade secrets

## Hook

A Google engineer downloaded 2,000+ pages of AI trade secrets — TPU chips, supercomputer designs — to his personal cloud. He was building an AI startup in China.

## Arc

Linwei Ding, 38, worked at Google as a software engineer. Between May 2022 and April 2023, he stole more than 2,000 pages of confidential information from Google's network and uploaded them to his personal Google Cloud account. The trade secrets: Google's custom Tensor Processing Unit (TPU) chips, GPU systems, the software that orchestrates thousands of chips into AI supercomputers, the SmartNIC card design. While stealing, he was secretly negotiating to be Chief Technology Officer of an early-stage PRC technology company. By early 2023, he was founding his own AI startup in China and pitching investors that he could "build an AI supercomputer by copying and modifying Google's technology." He applied for China's Thousand Talents Plan in late 2023. Ten days after stealing the final batch, he resigned from Google. A federal jury convicted him on all 14 counts — the first-ever conviction for AI-related economic espionage.
