# 05 — The NFL Player's $197M Medicare Fraud

**Status:** QUEUED
**Narrative score:** 7/10
**Source:** [DOJ press release, May 8, 2026](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/former-nfl-player-sentenced-over-16-years-prison-197m-medicare-fraud)
**Defendant:** Joel Rufus French, 47, Amory, Mississippi
**Court:** Federal court (specific district not stated in PR; convicted at jury trial Feb 2026)
**Sentence:** 196 months (16y 4mo) + $110,753,619 restitution + $17M forfeiture
**Conviction basis:** Six-day jury trial, conviction in February 2026 — conspiracy to commit health care fraud and wire fraud, conspiracy to commit money laundering, conspiracy to offer/pay/solicit/receive kickbacks

## Hook

A former NFL player built eight shell companies and a network of overseas call centers to bilk Medicare and disabled veterans' benefits of nearly $200M.

## Arc

French owned a marketing company and was the beneficial owner of eight durable medical equipment (DME) companies, all hidden behind straw owners. Working with overseas telemarketing call centers, he pressured elderly Americans into providing personal/insurance info and "agreeing" to medically unnecessary orthotic braces. In some cases, the call centers altered recordings to fake patient consent. He paid sham telemedicine companies for fraudulent doctors' orders from doctors who never met (and often never spoke to) the patients. Targeted Medicare and CHAMPVA (veterans' family healthcare). Laundered $225K in cash, including a personal courier run of $10K+ in a bag from Mississippi to Orlando.

## Key quotes

> "A former NFL player who owned a marketing company and was the beneficial owner of eight durable medical equipment (DME) companies was sentenced yesterday to 196 months in prison for his role in a yearslong scheme to bilk Medicare and the Civilian Health and Medical Program of the Department of Veterans Affairs (CHAMPVA) out of nearly $200 million by selling patient information and sham doctors' orders for orthotic braces that patients did not want or need."

> "French worked with overseas telemarketing call centers that pressured elderly Americans to provide their personal and health insurance information and agree to accept medically unnecessary orthotic braces. In certain instances, the call centers altered call recordings to make it seem like Medicare patients agreed to the braces when they did not."

> "French paid sham telemedicine companies kickbacks to obtain signed doctors' orders from doctors and nurse practitioners who never examined, and often never even spoke to, the patients."

> "French also laundered approximately $225,000 in cash from a bank in Mississippi, over $10,000 of which was placed in a bag and driven to Orlando to pay accomplices who sold him beneficiaries' personal and insurance information."
