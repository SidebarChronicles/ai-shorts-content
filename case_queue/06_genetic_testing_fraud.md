# 06 — The Half-Billion-Dollar DNA Scam

**Status:** QUEUED
**Narrative score:** 6/10
**Source:** [DOJ press release, May 4, 2026](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/two-sentenced-prison-522m-genetic-testing-fraud-and-illegal-kickback-scheme-targeting)
**Defendants:** Reyad Salahaldeen, 57 (Buford, GA) — ringleader; Mohamad Mustafa, 28 (Duluth, GA) — co-controller; 11 additional co-conspirators
**Sentences:** Salahaldeen 151 months; Mustafa 36 months; co-conspirators 6 mo–46 mo; one nurse practitioner 24 mo, two doctors 12 mo each
**Restitution:** Salahaldeen $84.6M + $3M forfeiture (2 bank accounts, 2019 GMC Yukon, TX + GA properties); Mustafa $64.3M
**Conviction basis:** Guilty pleas

## Hook

A four-lab operation paid kickbacks to door-to-door solicitors to harvest DNA from elderly Americans for unnecessary genetic tests — billing Medicare $522 million.

## Arc

From 2018–2020, Salahaldeen and Mustafa controlled four labs (Express Diagnostics NJ, BioConfirm GA, Tox Management TX, Tri-State Toxicology TX). They paid kickbacks/bribes to a network of "marketers" who:
- Targeted Medicare, Medicaid, and private-insurance beneficiaries
- Obtained DNA samples through telemarketing, **door-to-door solicitation**, health fairs, and in-person sales
- Got fraudulent lab requisition forms from doctors who never treated the patients
Salahaldeen falsified lab requisitions, letters of medical necessity, and other medical records. After indictment, he drove from North Carolina to Texas and tried to enter Mexico using someone else's ID — caught at the border. The labs billed $522M; insurers actually paid $84M.

## Key quotes

> "Reyad Salahaldeen, 57, of Buford, Georgia, was sentenced to 151 months in prison after pleading guilty to conspiracy to commit health care fraud and wire fraud."

> "From 2018 through August 2020, Salahaldeen and co-conspirators paid kickbacks and bribes to a network of purported 'marketers' who targeted individuals covered by Medicare, Medicaid, and private insurance and induced them to provide their health insurance information and DNA samples in order to obtain costly genetic tests designed to predict the risk of cancer, adverse drug reactions, and other conditions."

> "The marketers obtained DNA samples through telemarketing, door-to-door solicitation, appearances at health fairs, and other methods of in-person solicitation."

> "After the indictment was returned and he learned of the warrant for his arrest, Salahaldeen sought to evade law enforcement by traveling from North Carolina to Texas, where he attempted to enter Mexico. Salahaldeen was apprehended at the border when he falsely presented another person's identification in a further attempt to evade arrest and prosecution."

> "The four laboratories billed approximately $522 million in false and fraudulent claims, of which Medicare, Medicaid, and private insurers paid approximately $84 million."
