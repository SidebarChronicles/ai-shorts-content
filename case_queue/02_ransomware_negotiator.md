# 02 — The Ransomware Negotiator Who Sold Out His Clients

**Status:** QUEUED
**Narrative score:** 9/10
**Source:** [DOJ press release, April 20, 2026](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/florida-man-working-ransomware-negotiator-pleads-guilty-conspiracy-deploy-ransomware-and)
**Defendant:** Angelo Martino, 41, Land O'Lakes, FL
**Court:** Southern District of Florida
**Sentence:** Scheduled July 9, 2026 — faces up to 20 years
**Conviction basis:** Guilty plea, April 2026 — conspiracy to obstruct/affect commerce by extortion
**Co-defendants (pleaded guilty Dec 2025):** Ryan Goldberg (GA), Kevin Martin (TX) — sentencing April 30, 2026

## Hook

A ransomware negotiator at a cybersecurity firm was secretly feeding his clients' negotiating positions to the gang attacking them.

## Arc

Martino worked at a U.S. cyber incident response company. Five different clients hired his firm to negotiate ransom demands from BlackCat/ALPHV ransomware actors. He fed BlackCat each victim's insurance policy limits and internal negotiating strategy — they paid him for it. Then he joined the conspiracy outright and helped deploy ransomware himself, splitting a $1.2M Bitcoin ransom three ways with co-conspirators Goldberg and Martin. Feds seized $10M in his assets including digital currency, a luxury fishing boat, and a food truck.

## Key quotes from press release

> "Angelo Martino's clients trusted him to respond to ransomware threats and help thwart and remedy them on behalf of victims. Instead, he betrayed them and began launching ransomware attacks himself by assisting cyber criminals and harming victims, his own employer, and the cyber incident response industry itself." — AAG A. Tysen Duva

> "Working as a negotiator on behalf of five different ransomware victims, Martino provided BlackCat attackers with confidential information about the negotiating position and strategy of his company's clients without the clients' or his employer's knowledge or permission. This confidential information assisted the ransomware actors and maximized the ransoms that the victims were required to pay. The confidential information included the victims' insurance policy limits and internal negotiation positions. The BlackCat actors paid Martino for this confidential information."

> "After successfully extorting one victim for approximately $1.2 million in Bitcoin, the men split their share of the ransom three ways and laundered the funds through various means."

> "To date, law enforcement has seized $10 million of assets from Martino, including digital currency, vehicles, a food truck, and a luxury fishing boat."
